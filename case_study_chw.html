Cal Poly Health & Wellbeing · UX Designer · 2023 to 2025

Turning a neglected health portal into a trusted source of information.

For two years, I worked with clinicians, administrators, student teams, and campus stakeholders to improve how 23,000+ students found, understood, and acted on health information. It became less about one redesign, and more about slowly rebuilding trust in a system students had stopped relying on.

Research

Understanding why students stopped trusting the portal.

Before redesigning anything, I wanted to know why students weren’t using a resource meant to support them through some of their most important health decisions.

What kept showing up

01

Students search by task, not departmentBook an appointment, find testing, understand symptoms, figure out what’s next.

02

Dense content lowers trustLong paragraphs, clinical language, and inconsistent formatting made information harder to evaluate.

03

Small friction breaks trustOne broken link, outdated page, or buried action was often enough to make students leave.

What that meant for the work

The challenge wasn’t adding more content. It was helping students find, understand, and act on information with confidence.

What I created

The redesign became a system of pages, patterns, and content decisions.

Across two years, I redesigned and built service pages, navigation patterns, content structures, icons, and accessibility improvements.

01 · Scannable health information

Sensitive health pages students could actually understand.

For topics like STI testing, HIV testing, PrEP, and HPV prevention, I rebuilt dense clinical copy around plain language, short sections, accordions, and clear next steps.

02 · Action-oriented service pages

Critical actions, found before students gave up.

Students often knew what they needed, but the site hid the next step. I moved booking, forms, eligibility, and support to the top of key service pages.

03 · Inclusive resource discovery

Resources students struggled to find, brought forward.

With stakeholders, I restructured buried content like gender-affirming care and name and gender change resources around what students were trying to do.

04 · Consistent portal patterns

Reusable patterns inside a constrained CMS.

I designed reusable cards, accordions, section layouts, and icon treatments, so the portal felt cohesive and was easier to maintain.

Execution

Designing within the system we had.

The site ran on Drupal 7, so there were no modern design system tools or flexible components. The work was designing practical patterns that could actually be built in the CMS.

Reflection

What two years of stewardship taught me.

Trust isn’t built in one launch. It’s built through small moments: a clearer label, a working link, a warmer explanation, a page that actually answers the question a student came with.

01

Trust is built gradually.A health resource becomes reliable when every interaction shows it’s accurate, maintained, and useful.

02

Good content design reduces anxiety.In sensitive contexts, clear language and structure make the next step feel less intimidating.

03

Constraints can create better systems.Drupal 7 forced me to be deliberate, reusable, and realistic about what could scale.

04

Small improvements compound.The biggest impact came from improving the system again and again, not one big launch.
